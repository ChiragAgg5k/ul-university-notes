## MySQL reading note

Source: Riehle, D. (2012) ‘The single-vendor commercial open source business model’, *Information Systems and e-Business Management*, 10(1), pp. 5–17. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10257-010-0149-x) · [Author’s manuscript](https://dirkriehle.com/wp-content/uploads/2010/11/svcos-isebm-v05-final-final.pdf)

| Question | Evidence and location |
| --- | --- |
| Which model and case? | Code rights, dual licensing. Section 2 identifies MySQL as an example of licence sales. |
| Who could pay whom, and for what? | A company distributing its own product with an embedded database could pay the database vendor for commercial redistribution terms. Section 4.1 gives embedding as a buyer motive across firms. It does not identify a particular MySQL customer or transaction. |
| How does support differ? | Section 4.1 treats technical help and access to fixes as a separate purchase. The licence addresses what the buyer may distribute. Support addresses what happens when the software needs attention. |
| What follows for contributors? | Section 3.2 contrasts Olson’s account of copyright assignment with Augustin’s view that permission to relicense suffices. These accounts identify a condition on accepting outside code. The passage supplies no estimate of contributors lost. |
| When was the evidence collected? | The article synthesises practitioner accounts. Its references include a 2004 MySQL case (39) and a 2008 Mickos interview (26). These dates identify individual sources. Use 2012 for the article’s publication period in the coverage figure. |

Other reading leads: [Watson publication record](https://augusta.elsevierpure.com/p/en/research-outputs/the-business-of-open-source), [Han publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2025.107951), [Stanford case](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/mysql-open-source-database-2004).
